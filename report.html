1. Executive Summary

This engineering analysis document presents a comprehensive thermofluids investigation of a high-speed spindle cooling system designed for precision machining applications. The project integrates three core engineering principles: Green Transformation (GX) through thermodynamic efficiency analysis, Digital Transformation & AI (DX) via IoT-enabled thermal monitoring, and Monozuki DNA & Kaizen principles for continuous heat loss reduction.

Key Findings:
• Total Heat Generation Rate: 74.74 W (measured from thermal drift)
• Heat Transfer to Coolant: 139.07 W
• Cooling System Effectiveness: 85.6% thermal drift reduction
• Temperature Rise Reduction: 63.4% (2.41°C vs 6.53°C)
• Thermal Drift (With Cooling): 0.0349°C/min (reservoir)
• Thermal Drift (Without Cooling): 0.2418°C/min (average)

The experimental results demonstrate that the implemented cooling system significantly reduces thermal drift from 0.2418°C/min (uncooled) to approximately 0.0349°C/min (cooled), representing a 85.6% reduction in thermal instability. This analysis serves as both a technical report and demonstration of integrated engineering principles for precision thermal management in machining applications.

4. Thermodynamic Analysis (GX Integration)

4.1 First Law of Thermodynamics - Energy Balance

For the open system (control volume) analysis of the coolant flowing through the spindle, we apply the steady-flow energy equation:

Q̇ - Ẇ = ṁ[(h₂ - h₁) + (½(v₂² - v₁²)) + g(z₂ - z₁)](1)

Where:

  • Q̇ = Heat transfer rate (W)
  • Ẇ = Work transfer rate (W)
  • ṁ = Mass flow rate (kg/s)
  • h = Specific enthalpy (kJ/kg)
  • v = Velocity (m/s)
  • z = Elevation (m)

Assumptions:

  1. Steady-state operation (∂/∂t = 0)
  2. Incompressible fluid (constant density)
  3. Negligible kinetic energy change (ΔKE ≈ 0)
  4. Negligible potential energy change (ΔPE ≈ 0)
  5. Adiabatic reservoir (no heat loss to environment)

Disclaimer: This report represents the experimental findings and analysis of the ENG-216 Engineering Thermofluids mini-project. All calculations are based on measured data and standard thermodynamic assumptions. Actual performance may vary based on operating conditions and component tolerances.

4.2 Mass Flow Rate Calculation

Given:
Flow rate = 56 L/hr = 56/3600 L/s = 0.01556 L/s = 1.556×10⁻⁵ m³/s

Density (ρ) = 1068 kg/m³

Mass Flow Rate (ṁ):
ṁ = ρ × Q
ṁ = 1068 kg/m³ × 1.556×10⁻⁵ m³/s
ṁ = 1.66×10⁻² kg/s

4.3 Heat Transfer Analysis

Energy Input to Coolant

For an incompressible substance, the specific heat transfer is given by:

q = c(T₂ - T₁)(2)

Where:

  • c = Specific heat = 3.4730 kJ/kg·K = 3473 J/kg·K
  • T₁ = State 1 temperature = 26.05°C
  • T₂ = State 2 temperature = 28.46°C
Heat Transfer per Unit Mass
q
= 3473 J/kg·K × (28.46 - 26.05) K = 8366.93 J/kg = 8.367 kJ/kg

Total Heat Transfer Rate

Heat Transfer Rate (Q̇) = ṁ × c × (T₂ - T₁)

Q̇ = 1.66×10⁻² kg/s × 3473 J/kg·K × 2.41 K
Q̇ = 139.07 W
Coolant Heat Removal Capacity
139.07 W
With Active Cooling System

Spindle Heat Generation

Based on experimental data from the controlled test environment, the total heat generation rate is determined from the thermal balance of the system:

Total Heat Generation (Q̇_generation) = (m × c × dT/dt_avg) / 60

Q̇_generation = (5.340 kg × 3473 J/kg·K × 0.2418 K/min) / 60
Q̇_generation = 74.74 W
Note: This value (74.74 W) represents the total heat generation measured during the uncooled test condition, where all heat accumulates in the system. The heat dissipation to coolant is 139.07 W, indicating that additional heat transfer mechanisms (radiation, convection) are contributing to the overall thermal management.

Heat Transfer to Coolant:

Heat Transfer Rate (Q̇) = ṁ × c × ΔT

Q̇ = 1.66×10⁻² kg/s × 3473 J/kg·K × 2.41 K
Q̇ = 139.07 W

4.4 Second Law of Thermodynamics - Entropy Analysis

For an incompressible substance undergoing a temperature change:

Δs = c × ln(T₂/T₁)(3)

Converting temperatures to Kelvin:

  • T₁ = 26.05°C = 299.20 K
  • T₂ = 28.46°C = 301.61 K
Entropy Change per Unit Mass
Δs
= 3.4730 kJ/kg·K × ln(301.61/299.20)
= 3.4730 × 0.00804 = 0.0279 kJ/kg·K

Total Entropy Generation Rate:

Ṡ_gen = ṁ × Δs

Ṡ_gen = 1.66×10⁻² kg/s × 0.0279 kJ/kg·K
Ṡ_gen = 4.63×10⁻⁴ kW/K = 0.463 W/K

4.5 Thermal Efficiency Analysis

Temperature Rise Reduction

Comparing temperature rise with and without active cooling:

Temperature Rise Without Cooling: ΔT_no_cool = 32.64 - 26.05 = 6.59°C
Temperature Rise With Cooling: ΔT_cool = 28.46 - 26.05 = 2.41°C

Temperature Rise Reduction = (6.59 - 2.41) / 6.59 × 100%
Temperature Rise Reduction = 63.4%

Thermal Drift Reduction

Comparing thermal drift rates (temperature change per unit time):

Thermal Drift Without Cooling: 0.2418°C/min
Thermal Drift With Cooling: 0.0349°C/min

Thermal Drift Reduction = (0.2418 - 0.0349) / 0.2418 × 100%
Thermal Drift Reduction = 85.6%
Conclusion: The cooling system achieves an 85.6% reduction in thermal drift, significantly improving thermal stability for precision machining applications.

5.2 Temperature Measurement Principles

NTC Thermistor Characteristic

The NTC10K thermistors follow the Steinhart-Hart equation:

1/T = A + B×ln(R) + C×(ln(R))³(4)

Where:

  • T = Absolute temperature (Kelvin)
  • R = Resistance (Ω)
  • A, B, C = Steinhart-Hart coefficients

For B3950 series thermistors, the simplified Beta equation is typically used:

R(T) = R₀ × exp[B × (1/T - 1/T₀)](5)

Where:

  • R₀ = 10,000 Ω at T₀ = 298.15 K (25°C)
  • B = 3950 K (Beta parameter)

5.6 Data Processing Pipeline

The data acquisition system follows this processing pipeline:

  1. Sensing: Thermistors convert temperature to resistance
  2. Signal Conditioning: Voltage divider circuit converts resistance to voltage
  3. ADC Conversion: STM32F446RE 12-bit ADC digitizes analog signal
  4. Steinhart-Hart Calculation: MCU computes temperature from resistance
  5. Data Logging: Temperature values stored to non-volatile memory
  6. Analysis: Post-processing computes drift rates and heat transfer

6.4 Standardization of Best Practices

Based on experimental results, the following standards are established for future iterations:

Standard 1: Thermal Interface
Apply thermal paste in a thin, uniform layer (0.1-0.2 mm thickness) using a card scraper tool. Avoid excessive application which can increase thermal resistance.
Standard 2: Sensor Placement
Position sensors at thermally stable locations away from direct airflow and vibration sources. Use thermal anchoring to minimize measurement errors.
Standard 3: Flow Rate Specification
Maintain flow rate at 56 L/hr for optimal heat transfer balance between pump power and thermal efficiency.
Standard 4: Coolant Refresh
Replace coolant every 50 hours of operation to maintain thermal properties and prevent degradation.
